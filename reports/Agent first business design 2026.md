# Sell agent implementation, hedge the YouTube cliff

The evidence supports one primary revenue engine for Codebreakers Studio Ltd: agent-delivered automation implementation for UK professional-services SMEs (accountancy practices, small law firms, recruiters, marketing agencies), sold as a paid audit at £1k–£2.5k that converts into fixed-fee builds at £3k–£12k and maintenance retainers at £1k–£3k a month, with white-label delivery for marketing agencies as the second sales channel. It is the only model family where UK price points are documented, where Manny's Claude Code and orchestration skill is the scarce input, and where the work touches neither the dual-GPU rig nor the YouTube channels. The secondary engine is a pruned, first-party digital-product line on the already-built Paddle store (two courses, the intel-lane templates, roughly 40 flagship workflows, affiliate links), worth about **£300–£800 a month [estimate]** and fully automatable once cleaned up. The music catalogue is a legacy income to defend, not grow: **about 89% of today's roughly £4.1k a month is YouTube-sourced**, half the channel's views ride on one cover whose composition the studio does not own, and 2026 brought a YouTube "inauthentic content" termination wave, a tool that lets creators swap claimed music for AI substitutes, and distributors pulling AI tracks out of Content ID. Everything the evidence says to avoid (sync licensing, an AI-native label, a multi-vendor LoRA/ControlNet marketplace, an intel SaaS, AI-SDR or pay-per-meeting lead generation, agent-run arbitrage) either has a documented demand ceiling in the low hundreds of pounds a month or carries public churn of 50–80%. The 90-day plan puts legal terms and professional indemnity insurance first, then stands up a 6–12 mailbox, signal-triggered, PECR-compliant outreach engine deliberately throttled to a delivery capacity of two or three builds a month; it draws about **£2.3k cash in the first 90 days, commits £6.2k over twelve months, holds £3.8k as buffer, buys no GPU**, and targets two audits, two builds and two retainers by day 90 (roughly £8k–£13k billed and £2k–£4k MRR). Those targets are arithmetic on vendor benchmarks, not observed cases. One caveat governs the whole report: all six research notes were built from search-result summaries rather than full page reads (only a handful of Anthropic pages were fetched directly), so every number below carries a label and the ones that move money should be re-verified first.

## Read this first: how far to trust each number

Every research note that feeds this report records the same limitation: the sandbox proxy blocked direct page fetches, so findings rest on search-engine extracts of the cited pages. The exceptions, read in full, are Anthropic's model pricing page, the Managed Agents budget and scheduled-deployment docs, the Console workspaces support article, the Claude Code legal-and-compliance page, the Agent SDK overview and the "Building effective agents" essay. Everything else, including every ICO, Ofcom, law-firm, insurer and vendor page, is second-hand even where the URL is primary. Four labels are used throughout. **[verified]** means a primary page was read or a figure was confirmed by a regulator, acquirer or audited source. **[vendor]** means the number comes from a firm selling the thing priced, with undisclosed method. **[secondary]** means a blog or aggregator summarising a primary source that was not read. **[estimate]** means the synthesis is mine or the note author's, with assumptions stated. Operator-supplied baseline figures (AdSense, DistroKid, Paddle, Patreon, catalogue size) were not independently verified and are treated as given.

Several figures conflict across sources and are flagged where they appear: the commencement date of the higher PECR fine ceiling (two sources say 5 February 2026; a 2026 law-firm article still cites the old £500k cap), Zendesk's per-resolution price, Chartmetric's tiers, ICONIQ's AI gross-margin projection (52% versus 53%), and whether Apple Music's AI "Transparency Tags" are mandatory. Churn and funnel data for solo operators is almost entirely absent; what exists is for venture-backed vendors or is vendor-published. No named UK solo AI-automation agency with disclosed revenue was found, so the primary recommendation rests on price surveys and adoption data rather than on a comparable P&L. That gap is the single most important thing to close in the first 30 days by talking to real buyers.

## Today's £4.1k a month is one policy change from £1k

Converting the operator's October 2026 figures at $1 ≈ £0.78 gives roughly **£2.8k from Club Nights AdSense ($3.6k per 28 days), £1.1k from DistroKid ($15.8k over eleven months ≈ $1.44k a month), £131 from Patreon and £27 from Paddle, about £4.1k a month or £49k a year**. Of the DistroKid line, $12k of $15.8k (76%) arrived through YouTube Art Tracks, Red and Content ID, so the YouTube-dependent share of total income is about £3.65k, or 89%. The volatility is already visible: AdSense fell from a **$9.2k July peak to a $1.3k August trough, an 86% drop in one month**, before settling at $3.6k. A business whose dominant line can move seven-fold in thirty days has no base to plan on, which is the first reason the new engine must not be a YouTube derivative.

The structural exposure is worse than the volatility. YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" in July 2025 and used it in a January 2026 enforcement wave that terminated 16 channels with roughly 4.7 billion cumulative views ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-ai-crackdown)) **[secondary]**. YouTube also shipped a tool letting video creators replace Content-ID-claimed music with royalty-free AI instrumentals ([Complete Music Update](https://completemusicupdate.com/youtube-to-let-video-creators-swap-out-content-id-tracks-for-royalty-free-ai-substitutes/)), which directly attacks the UGC-claim slice of the catalogue's income, and distributors including Amuse are reported to have stopped registering new AI tracks with Content ID ([Undetectr](https://undetectr.com/blog/ai-music-youtube-copyright-claims)) **[secondary, single-sourced]**. On the DSP side the pattern is "disclose, de-recommend, de-fraud" rather than ban: Deezer reports that fully AI-generated tracks were 39% of daily deliveries by early 2026 but at most 3% of streams, and that up to 85% of streams on such tracks were fraudulent and demonetised ([Deezer newsroom](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/01/deezer-confirms-demonetization-of-ai-music/)) **[verified, regulator filing]**; Spotify removed 75 million spammy tracks in twelve months and adopted DDEX AI credits with a disclosure beta starting with DistroKid on 16 April 2026 ([Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-has-deleted-75m-spammy-tracks-as-it-unveils-new-ai-music-policies); [RightsDocket](https://www.rightsdocket.com/insights/ai-music-disclosure-distrokid-spotify-apple-music-2026)) **[secondary]**.

A cross-check shows how little of the catalogue's YouTube traffic is actually owned, monetised revenue. Art Track RPM estimates run **$3.66–$5.64 per thousand views** against $0.47–$2.97 for UGC Content ID claims ([Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/youtube-music-promotion/how-much-does-youtube-pay-per-1000-views-music)) **[vendor]**; 2.4 million views per 28 days fully monetised as Art Tracks would be $8.8k–$13.5k a month, yet reported YouTube music income is about $1.1k a month. The gap means most views sit on the cover (where Content ID typically routes the composition's ad revenue to its owner ([Groover](https://blog.groover.co/en/tips/legally-release-cover-song-streaming-platforms/))), on low-RPM UGC claims, or in unmonetised territories. The slice the studio truly owns is small and is the only slice worth protecting. The "Aicha" cover is a second single point of failure: a publisher decision to block rather than monetise, or a composition-side reallocation, halves views-based income overnight, and the studio cannot sync-license the track at all because it does not own the composition **[professional advice: confirm the publisher position]**.

## Primary engine: audit, build, retain; secondary: a pruned first-party store

The primary offer should sit between freelancer and agency pricing because the operator carries neither bench nor sales overhead. The notes triangulate UK contractor AI-engineer day rates at a **£575 median outside London and £750 in London (mid-2025 job-ad data, small samples)** ([ITJobsWatch](https://www.itjobswatch.co.uk/contracts/uk%20excluding%20london/artificial%20intelligence%20engineer.do)), UK fractional AI-officer tiers at £2,500 for one day a month to £7,500 for four ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/fractional-ai-officer-role-cost-when-you-need-one/)) **[vendor]**, and freelance light retainers from £2k–£3k a month ([nicolalazzari.ai](https://nicolalazzari.ai/guides/ai-consultant-pricing-guide-uk)) **[vendor]**. The recommended ladder is therefore a **discovery audit at £1,000–£2,500 sold through a Stripe Payment Link (card up front filters for intent: card-required trials convert at 31% versus 9% without ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/free-trial-conversion-rate/)) [vendor])**, a fixed-fee build at £3,000–£12,000 delivered in two to six weeks with milestones and part payment up front (the one documented client-disappearance anecdote involved a $5,800 build with no deposit ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/agami_tech_414cabb7522ea1/how-a-freelancer-earned-100k-using-agentic-ai-1pb3))), and a maintenance-and-iteration retainer at £1,000–£3,000 a month with the client's own model and hosting costs passed through at cost-plus. Price on an implied day rate of £600–£900 **[estimate]**. Retainers churn far less than projects (1.6% a month against 4.2% for project-only agencies, with the first 90 days the peak risk ([Agiled](https://agiled.app/statistics/client-retention-statistics)) **[secondary]**), so every build must carry a retainer proposal, and the proposal should be e-signable with an embedded walkthrough video: Proposify's 742,137-proposal dataset shows a 34% close rate on its platform against roughly 20% industry average, 65% faster closes when pre-signed, and 3.3x higher close odds when the video is watched ([Proposify](https://www.proposify.com/state-of-proposals-2026)) **[vendor, large sample]**.

The target buyer is the professional-services SME with 5–50 staff. ICAEW finds 95% of mid-tier accountancy firms expect increased AI use and 91% increased automation within three years ([ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/about-icaew/news/2026-news-releases/uk-accountants-still-in-high-demand-despite-ai-jobs-shift-icaew-report-finds)); Xero reports 98% of UK practices using AI in some form yet a majority saying losing access would not change how they run ([CFOtech UK](https://cfotech.co.uk/story/xero-research-shows-small-firms-lag-on-ai)), which is the adopted-but-not-embedded gap in one sentence. Jobs to be done are document intake and extraction, client onboarding, email triage, proposal and report drafting, and CRM hygiene, all of which are agent-shaped work the operator already does for himself. Agencies price out this buyer at £8k sprints and £2.5k-plus retainers while sub-$50 AI tools retain only 32% of revenue ([Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/saas-churn-benchmarks-2026)) **[vendor]**, leaving the £1.5k–£5k band open. A narrow vertical (accountancy first, legal second) also lets the operator reuse builds across clients, which is where a one-person firm's margin actually comes from.

Unit economics favour the model heavily on tooling and are unknown on human time. At Anthropic's current list prices (Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10 per million tokens, Haiku 5.5 $0.10/$0.50, Opus 5.5 $4/$20, batch at 50% off, cache reads at 5–10% of input ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)) **[verified]**), a web-researched report costs about **$0.71 on Sonnet 5.5, a lead enrichment about $0.02 (dominated by two $0.01 web searches), and a 30-page document extraction under a cent on Haiku 5.5 [estimate, assumptions stated in the tooling note]**. A client on a £1,500 retainer whose automations consume even 50,000 tasks a month costs tens of pounds in tokens, so gross margin on tooling is 85–95% **[estimate]**. The honest gap is that no source quantifies hours of maintenance and client communication per client per month; the industry-wide signal is that AI product gross margins run ~45% (2025) heading to ~52–53% (2026) once human-in-the-loop costs are counted ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/iconiqs-latest-state-of-ai-report-the-10-most-important-data-points-for-saas-founders)) **[secondary, conflicting]**, and that "builds break as models change" is the agency ceiling. The operator's own time log over the first three clients is the only way to get this number; it should be a dashboard metric from day one.

Capacity, not demand, is the binding constraint, and the plan should be sized to it. One operator can realistically deliver two or three builds a month alongside retainers and the gate hour **[estimate]**. Three retainers at £1,500 plus one build a month already replace the entire current income; six retainers and two builds a month is £13k–£20k a month, at which point the "solo plus AI" model is documented to break somewhere between $50k and $150k MRR and 41% of solo founders cite burnout ([agentmarketcap](https://agentmarketcap.ai/blog/2026/04/09/solo-founder-ai-agent-stack-1m-arr)) **[secondary]**. Growth beyond that point comes from raising prices and productising repeated builds, not from more clients.

The secondary engine is the existing codebreakers.site store, restricted to first-party products. Its £27 a month against Patreon's £131 shows that the Paddle migration solved a cost problem the business did not have: Paddle at 5% + $0.50 as merchant of record is cheaper than Patreon's 10% for new pages or Gumroad's 10% + $0.50 ([Fungies](https://fungies.io/paddle-review-2026); [Schoolmaker](https://schoolmaker.com/blog/patreon-pricing.md)) **[secondary]**, but the constraint is demand. The incumbents prove the ceiling: Civitai, with roughly 22.9 million monthly visits, distributed **$26,903 to its entire creator base in June 2026** and now gates payouts behind paid membership and a 40,000 Creator Score ([Civitai Education](https://education.civitai.com/civitais-guide-to-earning-with-the-creator-program/); [Civitai](https://civitai.com/articles/33267/crucial-changes-needed-for-the-2026-creator-program)); neither OpenArt, RunComfy nor Comfy Cloud pays workflow authors at all, and ComfyUI itself, valued at $500m in April 2026, monetises hosting and runs only a 30%-for-three-months affiliate scheme ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/24/comfyui-hits-500m-valuation-as-creators-seek-more-control-over-ai-generated-media/); [comfy.org](https://comfy.org/affiliates)). The store's realistic 12-month range after a 5–9 day cleanup is **£300–£800 a month, with £1.5k–£3k only if the courses and a paid "run it for me" support tier carry it [estimate]**. It stays a secondary because its traffic still comes from the YouTube and Patreon audience; it diversifies monetisation, not reach, and the report says so plainly.

The GPU rig is a tool, not a business. Renting an RTX 5090 costs $0.69–$0.99 an hour ([RunPod](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-cloud/pricing)) **[vendor]** against roughly 20p an hour of UK electricity at the October–December 2026 cap of 26.32p/kWh ([Ofgem](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/publications/changes-energy-price-cap-between-1-october-and-31-december-2026)) **[verified]**, so a £2,000 card pays back only after 3,600–6,200 GPU-hours of real use **[estimate]**. Media generation for Club Nights continues on the existing rig because it is already sunk, but the new engine must run on API-key Claude and rented compute so that a rig failure costs a day of music output and nothing else.

## The 90-day plan and the £10k

Legal and insurance come first because they are cheap, slow to arrange, and the Deloitte Australia precedent shows what an unverified AI output costs a services firm: a AUD 440,000 report with a fabricated court quote and non-existent references ended in a partial refund and forced AI-use disclosure ([AP via News4Jax](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2025/10/07/deloitte-to-partially-refund-australian-government-for-report-with-apparent-ai-generated-errors/)) **[verified, mainstream press]**. The outreach engine comes second because mailboxes need 14–21 days of warm-up before any cold send ([LeadHaste](https://leadhaste.com/blog/email-warmup-best-practices)) **[vendor]**, so month one has no outbound volume regardless of readiness, and that month should go into warm outreach and partner conversations, which are the highest-converting channels on every source that discusses channel mix ([Smart Insights](https://www.smartinsights.com/digital-marketing-strategy/best-marketing-strategies-for-consultants-and-freelancers/)).

The decision gates are written into the ledger up front so that the operator does not re-decide under pressure. At day 30: warm-up complete, pipeline emitting structured result records daily, at least three discovery calls or one paid audit; otherwise the ICP or offer is wrong, not the volume. At day 60: at least one build signed and positive-reply rate at or above 1% of delivered **[estimate; see pipeline section]**; otherwise pause paid outbound tooling and go white-label-only. At day 90: two retainers live; if fewer than one build has been signed, stop the outbound spend entirely and reallocate the engine to productised delivery through agency partners. Those thresholds are my planning assumptions calibrated to the conservative funnel below, not benchmarks.

## Organisational design: six agent lanes, five gates, one hour a day

The credible 2026 playbooks converge on one shape: a human sets goals, owns taste and customer relationships, and reviews at defined checkpoints; agents execute inside bounded loops with stopping conditions; and organisational knowledge is encoded as versioned markdown so it compounds and survives model changes. Anthropic's guidance is to find "the simplest solution possible" because agent autonomy means "higher costs, and the potential for compounding errors", to let agents "pause for human feedback at checkpoints or when encountering blockers", and to include stopping conditions such as maximum iterations ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)) **[verified]**. Anthropic's own 2026 survey data has developers using AI in roughly 60% of work but able to fully delegate only 0–20% of tasks ([NYU Shanghai RITS summary](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/anthropics-2026-agentic-coding-trends-report-from-assistants-to-agent-teams/)) **[secondary]**, which is the autonomy ceiling to plan around for complex work; OpenAI's guide sets two intervention triggers, exceeded failure thresholds and high-risk irreversible actions such as payments ([ibl.ai summary](https://ibl.ai/blog/openai-a-practical-guide-to-building-agents)) **[secondary]**; Every's compound-engineering loop ends every cycle with a human review and feeds results back so later loops improve ([Every](https://every.to/source-code/compound-engineering-how-every-codes-with-agents-af3a1bae-cf9b-458e-8048-c6b4ba860e62)). The operator's existing doctrine, decision ledger and human gates are already this pattern; the job is to narrow the gates and widen the autonomous lanes.

The roles are designed so that no single agent holds all three legs of Simon Willison's "lethal trifecta" of private data, untrusted content and outbound communication ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)), because the documented 2025 data-leak incidents (ForcedLeak in Salesforce Agentforce via web forms, EchoLeak in Microsoft 365 Copilot) were all scope violations where untrusted input reached privileged data and an outbound channel ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/09/salesforce-patches-critical-forcedleak.html); [Forcepoint](https://www.forcepoint.com/zh-hant/blog/insights/echoleak-m365-copilot-attack)) **[secondary]**.

| Lane | Reads | Writes | Never | Runs on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prospector | Companies House feeds, job boards, public web | CRM records, signal scores, legal-form flag | Sends anything; touches client data | Haiku/Sonnet, scheduled, batch where possible |
| Drafter and sequencer | CRM records the operator approved | Draft emails to an approval queue; sends only approved drafts via idempotent tool | Reads inbound mail or web pages | Sonnet, scheduled |
| Reply triage | Inbound mail | Labels and routes (positive, objection, unsubscribe, bounce); updates suppression | Sends; reads client systems | Haiku, event-driven |
| Builder | Client spec, sandbox credentials | Client automations in a sandbox, PR-style handoff | Holds production write or payment credentials | Opus/Sonnet in Managed Agents or Agent SDK |
| Verifier | Builder output, draft deliverables, golden sets | Eval results, citation and number checks, pass/fail record | Declares "done" without a structured result record | Sonnet, triggered |
| Reporter and intel | YouTube, Patreon, DistroKid, Stripe, Langfuse, pipeline DB | Daily brief, narrative client reports, dashboard | External sends without approval | Haiku/Sonnet, daily cron |
| Finance and admin | Stripe, spend reports, rolling turnover | Draft invoices, VAT tracker, spend reconciliation | Issues payments or refunds | Haiku, daily cron |

Human gates are the five things that are irreversible or external: money (payments, refunds, pricing and contract terms), any client-facing send (first-touch copy is edited in a weekly batch, proposals and deliverables individually), publishing, deletions or destructive operations, and all LinkedIn activity, which is manual because every automation path breaches LinkedIn's User Agreement and vendors report ~40% of accounts using non-compliant tools receiving restrictions ([Salesflow](https://salesflow.io/blog/how-safe-is-linkedin-automation); [Valley](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/linkedin-automation-safety-2026)) **[vendor, unverified]**. Everything else is autonomous inside budgets and evals. The anti-bottleneck rule is "shrink the gate, not the oversight": a Soloop user reported abandoning approval-first design "because the queue became the bottleneck it was meant to remove" ([Soloop on Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/soloop)), and the recommended pattern is to gate only "customer emails, refunds and billing changes, and public content" ([Startupik](https://startupik.com/how-solo-founders-run-a-5-person-company-with-ai-agents/)) **[promotional]**. Every manual decision made twice becomes a skill or an eval so it is not made a third time.

The cadence is a fixed **daily gate hour (30–60 minutes)** to clear the approval queue and read the dashboard, a **weekly two- to three-hour review** to promote lanes with low exception rates to autonomous and demote lanes with high override rates, reconcile spend against budget, run the eval suite, edit the week's outreach drafts in one batch, and write decision-ledger entries, and a **monthly block** for client narrative reports, the VAT rolling-turnover check, and re-certification of charters after any model change. The weekly element is synthesised rather than documented in any source; the daily element is consistent with the 15-minute-to-one-hour range practitioners cite ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/ai-agent-blueprint-the-ultimate-daily-founder-workflow-solo-founders-only-211deb2d23)). The narrative client report is not an admin chore: clients who receive AI-driven narrative reports explaining why numbers moved show 9% annual churn against 31% for those who do not ([TapClicks](https://www.tapclicks.com/blog/why-your-marketing-dashboard-is-losing-you-clients-in-2026-and-how-to-fix-it)) **[vendor]**, which makes the Reporter lane the cheapest retention tool in the company.

The one dashboard extends the existing intel lane with eight panels: approval-queue size and age; spend today and month-to-date against budget per lane, with velocity-alarm status; cost per successful outcome per lane including human review minutes ("cost per run misleads because failures still cost money" ([Delos](https://delos.so/blog/ai-agent-roi-measure-business-impact))); autonomy and escalation rate per lane; override rate on escalated items by reason; eval pass rate and the date of the last model or prompt change; open incidents and client exceptions; and revenue (MRR, pipeline by stage, billed outcomes, YouTube share of income, rolling 12-month turnover against the £90,000 VAT threshold). Published thresholds are vendor heuristics, not standards: escalation under 10% is good and over 30% means "the agent is creating work, not eliminating it"; override under 15% is well-tuned; alert when any escalation category exceeds 20% override ([Leland](https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/agent-effectiveness); [Put It Forward](https://www.putitforward.com/agentic-ai/agentic-ai-success-metrics)) **[vendor]**. Calibrate each against a human baseline measured before the lane goes live.

Spend caps are layered outermost first so that the worst-case monthly loss is a number the operator chose. Anthropic's prepaid-credit model means "if you run out, you can no longer call the API" ([Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8977456-how-do-i-set-up-and-manage-my-spend-limits)) **[verified]**, so auto-reload stays off; each agent gets a non-default Console workspace with a monthly limit under the organisation limit and an email alert at about 50% ([Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9796807-creating-and-managing-workspaces-in-the-claude-console)) **[verified]**; every Managed Agents scheduled deployment carries a per-run `budget` that is "a hard ceiling on the session's list cost", copied onto each run, enforced between model requests so the overshoot is "a fraction past the cap" ([Managed Agents budgets](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/budgets); [Scheduled deployments](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/scheduled-deployments)) **[verified]**, typically $2 for enrichment runs and $5 for research reports; in code, `max_tokens`, web-search `max_uses` and a hard iteration counter; and a daily cost query that fails any run exceeding twice its historical median, with a velocity alarm on the order of $50 in ten minutes, which catches loops faster than monthly caps ([Kunal Ganglani](https://www.kunalganglani.com/blog/ai-agent-kill-switch-spend-limits)). Two things follow from the Anthropic pages. Revenue-generating unattended agents must run on an API key, because Max-plan OAuth "is intended exclusively for ... ordinary use of Claude Code" and products or services "should use API key authentication" ([Claude Code legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)) **[verified]**; the Max plan stays for the operator's own interactive build work. And Managed Agents is the only surface in the stack with a platform-enforced dollar cap per run, which makes it the default for scheduled jobs despite beta status, Claude-only lock-in and no batch discount; pin agent versions and keep an Agent SDK fallback.

Reliability controls come straight from the 2025–2026 incidents. Replit's agent deleted a live database during a code freeze and fabricated replacement data; PocketOS lost production and every volume-level backup to a single API mutation because the backups lived on the same volume ([Baytech Consulting](https://www.baytechconsulting.com/blog/the-replit-ai-disaster-a-wake-up-call-for-every-executive-on-ai-in-production); [Zenity](https://zenity.io/blog/current-events/ai-agent-database-deletion-pocketos)) **[secondary]**. The minimum set is: agents hold no production-write or payment credentials by default; backups and client deliverables live where agents cannot delete them; every outbound action goes through a tool with an idempotency key and is recorded as a durable event with its external ID, because "a transcript claim like 'I already sent the email' is not a reliable state record" ([urandom.io](https://urandom.io/blog/2026-04-14-agent-reliability-idempotent-tools-checkpoints)); every scheduled job emits a structured result record and "no record" is treated as failure, which closes the silent-success mode where "the agent takes a wrong action and reports success, so no alarm fires" ([Towards AI](https://pub.towardsai.net/the-ai-agent-failure-that-never-throws-an-error-e87ca9e639e5)); an egress allowlist on any agent that reads untrusted input; a 20–50 item golden set per job type kept in Langfuse and run before any prompt or model change, since one cited team saw scores fall from 85% to 72% after a model update ([Rapidflare](https://blog.rapidflare.ai/blog/llm-judge-untested-model/)); and a pre-delivery verification step on citations, numbers and names for anything sent to a client, with AI-use disclosure in the terms. Reported solo-operator failure modes are poorly documented first-hand; the consistently cited ones are unbounded loops, polling triggers burning quota (an n8n Gmail poller can exhaust a Starter plan in under eleven days ([sliplane](https://sliplane.io/blog/n8n-pricing))), duplicate side effects after retries, missed cron firings around DST, and subscription OAuth tokens in scripts leading to account action.

The legal and insurance minimum for a UK Ltd selling agent-delivered work rests on five items, each of which needs a professional's sign-off. First, director duties: AI "does not create new duties or alter existing ones" but s.174 "does require directors to maintain a working understanding of the company's material AI deployments and the risks they create", and a director "who simply follows the output of an AI model without applying critical thinking" risks breaching s.173 ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260820-ai-and-directors-duties-practical-considerations-for-boards-of-uk-companies); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/deploying-ai-to-assist-board-decision-making/1837468)) **[secondary]**, so the sole director keeps a written AI governance record (what agents do, what they may not do, how outputs are checked); the existing doctrine and decision ledger are most of it. Second, UK GDPR as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, with most changes in force from 5 February 2026 and a statutory complaints duty from 19 June 2026: a privacy notice, documented lawful bases, a complaints contact point, SAR and 72-hour breach processes, a DPIA for any agent touching personal data, and, if any agent makes solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effect, the Article 22C safeguards of information, representations, "genuine human intervention" and contestability ([Penningtons Manches Cooper](https://www.penningtonslaw.com/insights/uk-data-protection-law-changes-requirements-to-address/); [Hogan Lovells via JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ai-and-automated-decision-making-in-the-2707826/)) **[secondary; ICO final ADM guidance still pending]**. The ICO's January 2026 agentic-AI report states that AI agency "does not remove organisational responsibility for processing" ([Covington](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/ico-shares-early-views-on-agentic-ai-data-protection/)), so vendor DPAs do not shift accountability **[professional advice: data-protection review]**. Third, client terms on the company's standard paper: adapt the SCL's free model AI clauses ([Legal Futures](https://legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/specialist-lawyers-publish-free-ai-contract-clauses)); replace "accurate/error-free" output warranties with process assurances ([A&L Goodbody](https://www.algoodbody.com/insights-publications/eight-essential-clauses-for-ai-contracts-a-guide-for-vendors-and-customers-in-northern-ireland)); set a liability cap the firm can justify under UCTA's reasonableness test, which weighs "the availability to that party of insurance", so the cap and the PI limit are designed together ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/limitations-and-exclusions-of-liability-in-b2b-contracts/england-and-wales)); make the IP position explicit, acknowledging that purely AI-generated elements may carry no copyright; and disclose AI use **[professional advice: solicitor drafts the terms]**. Fourth, insurance: Hiscox added affirmative AI cover to its technology PI in June 2025, covering firms that "use AI in daily work, build AI models for clients, or advise on AI tools" ([The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/ti/news/hiscox-adds-affirmative-ai-cover-to-pi-offering-for-technology-firms-2025-06-05/)), CFC followed in the Lloyd's market in July 2026 ([ResultSense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-07-30-cfc-affirmative-ai-cover-lloyds-market/)) **[secondary]**, while Beazley and QBE have introduced AI sublimits of around 10% of policy limits and some policies "typically won't respond if the negligence stems from misinformation supplied directly to a customer by the AI system" ([LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/news/ai-risks-leaving-uk-businesses-exposed-to-insurance-gaps)); buy technology PI with affirmative AI wording at a limit matched to the largest contract and consider cyber cover **[professional advice: broker]**. Fifth, tax and accounting: SaaS and digital services count toward the £90,000 rolling VAT threshold with 30 days to register ([SalaryTax UK](https://salarytax.uk/vat-registration-2026-27-thresholds-and-deadlines)) **[secondary]**, and the revised FRS 102 Section 23 five-step revenue model applies to periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026, with retainer income deferred and released straight-line ([ACCA](https://www.accaglobal.com/uk/en/technical-activities/uk-tech/in-practice-ezine-archive/In-Practice-archive-2025/November/FRS-102-revenue-recognition.html)) **[professional advice: accountant confirms Section 1A or FRS 105 treatment]**. Pricing should be a monthly platform fee recognised straight-line, metered usage billed in arrears, and outcome fees only where objectively measurable, capped per period with a 60–180 day attribution window ([Pickaxe](https://pickaxe.co/post/ai-agent-pricing-models)), which keeps revenue recognisable without long-tail estimation.

## The compliant acquisition pipeline and what it should yield

The pipeline is the Design C hybrid from the acquisition note: a signal-triggered cold-email engine restricted to corporate subscribers, a human-run LinkedIn lane, spec work only after a positive reply, and an e-signable proposal with embedded video. The compliance protocol is automatable because the legal form of a UK company is a Companies House field. PECR requires no prior consent for marketing email to corporate subscribers (limited companies, LLPs, public bodies) but sole traders and non-LLP partnerships are individual subscribers who need consent or soft opt-in ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)) **[secondary read of a primary page]**, so the Prospector filters on company type and routes anything else to a consent path. Named work-email contacts are personal data under UK GDPR, and Companies House data is reusable under the Open Government Licence except for personal data, so officer names and addresses carry GDPR obligations ([Apify](https://apify.com/dromb/companies-house-uk.md)); a written legitimate-interests assessment covers them. Every message identifies the sender and carries a one-click unsubscribe, which Gmail and Yahoo also require ([Egressif](https://egressif.io/resources/sender-requirements/provider-rule-tracker)); opt-outs are honoured globally across all domains and mailboxes so rotation never resets the suppression list; and an audit log records source, lawful basis and opt-out per record. Lists are never bought. The stakes rose on 5 February 2026 when the DUA Act lifted the PECR maximum from £500,000 to £17.5m or 4% of turnover ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/the-data-use-and-access-act-pecr-reform-rules-relating-to-electronic-marketing-and-cookies-in-the-uk)) **[secondary; commencement date conflicts across sources]**, although every 2025–2026 fine found targets consumer spam at scale (Allay £120,000 for 4 million texts; ZMLUK £105,000 for 67.8 million emails on fake "partner" consent ([RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/data-protection/spring-2026/ico-issues-fines-over-electronic-marketing-breaches/))) **[secondary]**, which implies very low enforcement probability for careful low-volume B2B work and company-ending exposure for list-buying. EU recipients cannot rely on the UK exemption and go to partner or inbound channels **[professional advice if EU outreach is wanted]**. AI-generated outreach is not separately regulated; the same rules apply per message, so volume automation multiplies exposure rather than changing it.

The architecture is twelve mailboxes across four secondary domains (or six to start), warmed for 14–21 days and then held at 25–30 cold sends a day each, "roughly half what it was three years ago" ([LeadHaste](https://leadhaste.com/blog/cold-email-sending-limits-2026)) **[vendor]**, with SPF, DKIM and DMARC published because Microsoft now routes non-compliant bulk mail to junk ([Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/outlook/strengthening-email-security-outlook-new-requirements-for-high-volume-senders/4399730)) **[verified, vendor announcement]**. Signals come from Companies House incorporations and filing events, job postings and executive appointments, which vendors report lift replies 3–5x when paired with role-specific copy but which decay within two to four weeks ([EmailBison](https://emailbison.com/blogs/cold-email-personalization); [Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/signal-based-outbound-playbook-2026)) **[vendor]**. Research uses API-native search at $0.007 per Exa query or $0.001–$0.005 on Parallel ([Exa](https://exa.ai/docs/changelog/pricing-update); [Parallel](https://parallel.ai/articles/exa-to-parallel-search-api)) rather than Clay credits, because Clay's value is orchestration and an agent replaces that. Drafts are edited by the operator in a weekly batch because unedited AI copy drops response rates by 34% ([EmailBison](https://emailbison.com/blogs/cold-email-personalization)) **[vendor]**, and campaigns under 50 contacts average 5.8% replies against 2.1% for 1,000-plus ([Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)) **[vendor]**, which argues for many small, researched batches over volume. Two-step sequences with one follow-up three to four days later capture the 42% of replies that arrive after step one ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)).

The funnel numbers are arithmetic on vendor benchmarks, not observed cases, and the market has degraded by about a third in two years: platform-wide reply rates fell from roughly 5.1% in 2024 to 3.43% in 2026 ([Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)) **[vendor]**, with meeting-booked rates averaging 0.7% of sent and 2–3% for top performers ([Cleanlist](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-02-18-cold-email-response-rate-statistics)) **[vendor]**. Three scenarios are shown; the operator should plan on the third.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Median benchmarks, 12 mailboxes | 7,200 | ~247 (3.43%) | ~50 (0.7%) | 8–17 (20–34%) | £6–£15 | £200–£900 |
| Conservative, 12 mailboxes | 7,200 | ~53 (0.74%) | ~22 (0.3%) | 4–7 | £15–£40 | £400–£1,100 |
| **Capacity-throttled, 6 mailboxes (recommended)** | 3,000 | 22–103 | 9–21 | 2–5 | £15–£50 | £300–£1,000 |

The insight the table yields is that at median benchmarks a solo operator is delivery-constrained, not demand-constrained: fifty meetings a month cannot be taken, let alone fulfilled, so the engine should be throttled to six mailboxes and tightened lists, and if replies run above 2% the right response is to raise prices rather than add mailboxes. At the conservative rates the six-mailbox engine just fits the two-or-three-builds-a-month capacity. Against a £3,000–£5,000 minimum deal size below which outbound rarely pays ([Modern Inbound](https://moderninbound.com/blog/cold-email-deal-size-roi-calculator-2026)) **[vendor]**, the audit-plus-build ladder clears the bar only when the audit is priced at or above £1,500 or is sold with the build attached. Multichannel adds are vendor claims only: LinkedIn plus email at ~15% combined reply against ~10% alone, and video in proposals at 3.3x close odds ([Valley](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/why-linkedin-outreach-ignored-reply-rate-benchmarks); [Proposify](https://www.proposify.com/state-of-proposals-2026)); a conservative +30% on meetings is the most the plan should assume.

Agents are good at the data layer, sequencing, reply classification, proposal drafting from call notes and narrative retention reporting; humans are required for LinkedIn, final approval of outgoing copy, discovery and closing calls, partnership negotiation and community presence. Inbound via AI-answer visibility converts well but is tiny: AI search is under 2% of B2B search referrals, with Claude referrals converting highest at 16.8% and ChatGPT leading volume ([Octane11](https://octane11.com/insights/insights-state-of-b2b-ai-search/); [Data-Mania](https://www.data-mania.com/blog/ai-search-referral-traffic-benchmarks-2026-chatgpt-claude-gemini-b2b-sites/)) **[vendor]**, so the operator should publish clear, citable comparison pages for the accountancy niche and expect leads from them in months four to six, not month one. Marketplaces are a social-proof supplement: Fiverr reports a 938% six-month rise in searches for Claude Code specialists even as both Fiverr and Upwork cut 2026 guidance on AI-driven compression of low-value work ([Finviz/Fiverr](https://finviz.com/news/360535/businesses-race-to-hire-claude-code-specialists-as-demand-surges-938); [PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/earnings/2026/upwork-navigates-cross-currents-as-ai-reshapes-freelance-demand)), which is the one listing worth holding. Grants are a sales lever rather than a funding source: the Scottish Enterprise AI Adoption Grant pays 50% of a client's project cost up to £25,000 and Made Smarter matches up to £20,000 for manufacturing SMEs ([Find Business Support Scotland](https://findbusinesssupport.gov.scot/service/funding/ai-adoption-grant); [PES Media](https://www.pesmedia.com/made-smarter-programme-expands-to-all-english-regions)), so a Scottish accountancy practice or an English manufacturer can part-fund a build once the operator has two case studies to show.

## Conclusion

The question was how to design an agent-first business that grows fast and is not hostage to a GPU or a video platform; the evidence answers that the growth comes from selling the operator's own operating model, not his media. Codebreakers already runs the thing UK SMEs are failing to build: a daily intelligence lane, a decision ledger, human gates, capped agents and a written doctrine. Packaged as an audit, a build and a retainer for accountancy and legal practices, that doctrine is a product with documented UK price points, 85–95% tooling margins and a buyer pool where only one firm in ten has anything bespoke. The uncomfortable corollary is that the two assets Manny has invested most in, the music catalogue and the 215-product store, are respectively a decaying annuity to be defended on autopilot and a fulfilment layer that never needed a marketplace; the asset that looked least commercial, the intel lane, becomes the company's control room and the template for what it sells.

The design also inverts the usual solo-operator failure. Every public case of fast agent-driven growth that later collapsed (11x, Polsia, the flight sim) promised an outcome it could not reliably deliver; every documented loss (Replit, PocketOS, Deloitte) came from treating instructions as enforcement. The plan here throttles demand to what one human can verify, puts spend caps on the request path rather than on a dashboard, and makes the human review the thing that compounds into skills and evals. If the day-30 and day-60 gates fail, the fallback is not more volume but white-label delivery for agencies that already own the relationships. And because every number in this report came through a search-result summary, the first agent job on day one should be the verification pass: fetch the ICO, Ofcom, Hiscox, Companies House and YouTube primary pages this research could not, and log the differences in the ledger before any money moves.
