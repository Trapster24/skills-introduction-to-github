## Key Question 1: Realistic 2026 funnel benchmarks (sent → replies → meetings → closes) for AI-personalised B2B outreach, and how much they have degraded since 2024

### Takeaway
Platform-wide cold-email reply rates in 2026 cluster at roughly 2–3.5% of emails sent (Instantly/Woodpecker 3.43%, Saleshandy 3.7%, Cleanlist 3.1%, Sales.co 2.09%, Smartlead 0.74%, Belkins strict 0.45%), down from ~5.1% in 2024 on the most-cited series — a ~33% decline in two years. Meeting-booked rates average ~0.5–1% of sent, with 2–3% for top performers. Signal-triggered, genuinely researched emails claim 8–19% replies; unedited AI copy performs worse than templates.

### Cited Findings

**Reply-rate levels (2026)**
- Instantly's 2026 benchmark report covers "billions of cold email interactions" Jan–Dec 2025; platform-average reply rate 3.43%, top quartile 5.5%, top 10% above 10.7% — [Instantly Cold Email Benchmark Report 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026); figures also summarised in [Lemlist](https://www.lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks) and [Instantly sequence benchmarks](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/)
- Woodpecker (20M+ emails) reports the same 3.43% platform average in its June 2026 update — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Saleshandy analysis of 53M emails: 3.7% average reply — summarised by [Instantly/Lemlist](https://www.lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks)
- Cleanlist: 3.1% average reply, top performers 8–12% — [Cleanlist](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-02-18-cold-email-response-rate-statistics)
- Sales.co study: 2.09% across 2M+ emails — cited in [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/cold-email-statistics)
- Smartlead's agency-pricing worked example uses 0.74% reply rate (1 reply per 135 contacts) as its benchmark — [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/cold-email-agency-pricing)
- Belkins strips auto-replies and measures against total sent; its strict figure is 0.45% — summarised in [EmailBison](https://emailbison.com/blogs/cold-email-open-rates); but Belkins' own 16.5M-email/93-domain analysis is elsewhere summarised as 8.4% reply on the first email and 6.8% (2023) → 5.8% (2024) — [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/cold-email-response-rate). These figures use different denominators/years and are not comparable. Belkins' State of B2B Outreach dataset: 7.5M+ emails, 175k cold calls, 15M+ LinkedIn touchpoints — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/b2b-outreach)
- One vendor puts "meaningful" replies (ex-bounce/OOO) at ~2.1% — [EmailBison](https://emailbison.com/blogs/cold-email-open-rates)

**Degradation since 2024**
- Woodpecker: platform-wide reply rate declined from 5.1% in 2024 to 3.43% in 2026, attributed to inbox saturation, stricter spam filtering and AI-generated outreach — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Secondary sources attribute the same 5.1% (2024) → 3.43% (2025/26) series to Instantly data — [Serpsculpt](https://serpsculpt.com/average-cold-email-response-rate-b2b/); [Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/b2b-cold-email-statistics-2026)
- Cleverly: ~3.4% in 2026, down from ~5% in 2025 and 8.5% in 2019 — [Cleverly](https://cleverly.co/blog/cold-email-statistics)
- Belkins series: 6.8% (2023) → 5.8% (2024) — [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/cold-email-response-rate)
- One blog citing Gong's 2025 State of Revenue puts cold B2B replies at 1–3% in 2026, down from 3–5% in 2022 (attribution unverified) — [Boomerang](https://getboomerang.ai/glossaries/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks-2026)
- Commentary claims reply-rate decay above 60% within 18 months for fully AI-run outbound; the underlying analysis is unnamed — [eChai](https://echai.ventures/gtm/gtm-outbound/we-handed-outbound-to-ai-and-reply-rates-fell-where-does-ai-actually-help-and-where-does-it-backfire)

**Meeting rates**
- Cleanlist: overall average meeting-booked rate 0.7% of sent; 2–3% for top performers — [Cleanlist](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-02-18-cold-email-response-rate-statistics)
- RevenueFlow: 0.5–1.5% meeting rate for average B2B campaigns; SMB 1–2.5%, lower for enterprise — [RevenueFlow](https://www.revenueflow.com/blog/cold-email-conversion-benchmarks)
- Smartlead worked example: 0.74% reply → ~$467 per held meeting (later funnel steps are stated assumptions) — [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/cold-email-agency-pricing)

**Personalisation / AI effects**
- Woodpecker: advanced-personalised emails average ~18% replies vs ~9% for basic templates (older version: 17% vs 7%) — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Woodpecker: campaigns under 50 contacts average 5.8% replies vs 2.1% for 1,000+ (a third-party summary uses a 500+ cutoff) — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Saleshandy (via EmailBison): 34% drop in response rate for campaigns relying on unedited AI-generated copy — [EmailBison](https://emailbison.com/blogs/cold-email-personalization)
- Coffee.ai (vendor): Q1 2026 analysis of 11,000 first-touch emails — signal-referencing emails 19.4% reply vs 3.1% for merge-field personalisation; method undisclosed — [Coffee.ai](https://coffee.ai/articles/cold-email-software-ai-personalization)
- Autobound/Komo: ~18% response for signal-referencing outreach vs 3.4% cold average (~5x) — [Autobound](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/b2b-prospecting-signals-guide); [Komo](https://komo.ai/glossary/hiring-signal)
- Growleads data across 200 B2B campaigns (via Vantaige): 8–15% replies for trigger-based campaigns — [Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/signal-based-outbound-playbook-2026)
- EmailBison: job postings, exec appointments and headcount growth produce 3–5x reply lift when paired with role-specific messaging (loosely attributed to Forrester) — [EmailBison](https://emailbison.com/blogs/cold-email-personalization)
- One source rates job postings the weakest of five signals at +25% lift, versus funding and pricing-page visits — [Darwin](https://blog.getdarwin.ai/en/ai-cold-email-2026-signal-based-strategies-reply-rates)
- Signal decay: most signals decay within 2–4 weeks ([Vantaige](https://vantaige.io/blog/signal-based-outbound-playbook-2026)); Komo gives 7–90 days depending on signal type and says new-role creation carries 2–3x the intent of backfill hires — [Komo](https://komo.ai/glossary/hiring-signal)
- Warm vs cold: AE warm sends reply at 28.38% vs 8.98% for SDR cold sends (vendor compilation) — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/cold-email-statistics)

**Sequence structure**
- Instantly: 58% of replies arrive on step 1, 42% from later steps — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026); Woodpecker: 42% of replies come from follow-ups, 48% of reps never send one — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)
- Belkins reportedly stopped tracking open rates entirely across 16.5M emails (Apple MPP inflation) — [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/cold-email-open-rate); Woodpecker also flags open-rate inflation — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)

**Gong**
- No Gong Labs 2026 study on AI-written outbound was found. Gong's product marketing claims Gong Engage users see 34% higher response rates and cites one customer rising from 16% to 30% — [Gong](https://www.gong.io/ai-generated-sales-emails)

### Inferences
- A defensible planning range for a solo operator with good targeting and human-edited AI drafts: 3–6% total replies, of which roughly one-third to one-half are positive (positive-reply share is NOT published by the major reports; this split is an inference), and 0.7–1.5% of sent becoming a booked meeting. The 15–19% "signal-based" figures are small-sample vendor claims and should be treated as an upper bound achievable only on tiny, hand-curated lists (consistent with Woodpecker's <50-contact 5.8% finding).
- The decline from ~5% (2024) to ~3.4% (2026) is about one-third in two years; vendor consensus attributes it partly to AI-generated volume, which implies the marginal return on "more AI emails" is negative at the market level and the operator should compete on list quality and timing, not volume.
- Because Belkins' strict denominator yields 0.45% versus Instantly's 3.43%, operators should define their own metric (positive human replies ÷ delivered) before comparing against any benchmark.

### Gaps
- No report publishes a clean positive-reply → meeting → close chain on one dataset; the chain below is stitched from different vendors.
- Could not access Instantly's or Belkins' reports directly to confirm dataset sizes or the 2024 comparison; the 5.1% figure is second-hand.
- No independent (non-vendor) 2026 study of AI-personalised vs human-written cold email was found.
- No Gong Labs 2026 outbound data was found.

## Key Question 2: Which acquisition channels agents are genuinely good at in 2026, and which still need a human

### Takeaway
Agents are demonstrably good at the data layer (sourcing from Companies House and public registries, enrichment via Apollo/Clay/Exa/Parallel, signal detection from job posts and filings), at drafting and sequencing email within sending limits, and at proposal/document generation. LinkedIn automation is a terms-of-service breach with meaningful restriction risk; closing conversations, judgement on positive replies, partnerships and community still need a human. Inbound via AI-answer visibility converts well but is tiny in volume.

### Inferences
- Agent-suitable (high confidence from evidence): registry/job-board/review-site sourcing, enrichment waterfalls, signal detection and scoring, drafting personalised first-touch emails for human approval, sequencing and reply classification, proposal drafting from call notes, narrative reporting for retention (the 9% vs 31% churn figure directly rewards automated narrative QBR reporting).
- Human-required: LinkedIn activity (every automation path breaches ToS and vendor-reported restriction rates are high; a solo operator cannot afford to lose the one account), final approval of outgoing copy (the 34% penalty for unedited AI copy), discovery/closing calls, partnership negotiation, and community participation.
- Inbound via AI answers is a conversion-quality channel, not a volume channel, for at least 2026: at <2% of search referrals, a small firm should build for it (clear, citable pages; comparison content) but cannot plan a pipeline around it.
- Per-record economics now favour API-native search (Exa $0.007/search, Parallel $0.001–0.005) over Clay credits for a technically strong operator; Clay's value is orchestration, which an agent can replace.

## Key Question 3: Productised services with self-serve checkout vs proposal-based selling for AI automation work (incl. spec work / free-sample-first)

### Takeaway
No published data directly compares self-serve checkout for AI automation services against proposal-based selling; the closest analogues are SaaS trial-to-paid (median ~8%; 12% sales-assisted; card-required trials 31% vs 9% no-card) and proposal close rates (~20% industry, 34% on Proposify). Loom/audit "spec work" openers are widely recommended with self-reported 15% reply rates but no controlled evidence.

### Cited Findings

- Paddle's merchant-of-record fee (~5% + FX) is only worth paying if selling meaningfully into the EU/US where VAT/sales-tax registration would otherwise be required; a UK-only or mostly-UK seller should use Stripe Payment Links + Stripe Tax.

## Key Question 4: Compliant automated B2B outreach from the UK (PECR corporate-subscriber rules, opt-out, ICO fines 2025/2026)

### Takeaway
PECR does not require prior consent for marketing email to corporate subscribers (limited companies, LLPs, public bodies), but sole traders and non-LLP partnerships are individual subscribers requiring consent or soft opt-in; every message must identify the sender and give a working opt-out, and UK GDPR still needs a lawful basis (legitimate interests) for named individuals. The DUA Act 2025 lifts the PECR fine ceiling from £500k to £17.5m/4% turnover; 2026 fines so far (Allay £120k, ZMLUK £105k) target consumer spam, not B2B.

### Cited Findings
- ICO: no prior consent requirement for marketing emails to corporate subscribers; ICO table lists email to corporate subscribers as not requiring consent; corporate subscribers include limited companies, LLPs and public bodies — [ICO B2B marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/); [ICO at a glance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing/business-to-business-marketing)
- Sole traders and non-LLP partnerships (England, Wales, NI) are individual subscribers; consent or soft opt-in applies — [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/); [Harper James](https://harperjames.co.uk/article/b2b-marketing-and-gdpr-the-basics/); [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/b2b-email-marketing-rules-uk/)
- Disputed reading: Warner Goodman reports ICO staff saying B2B marketing to an identifiable business address needs neither consent nor soft opt-in under PECR or UK GDPR, but argues a lawful basis is still needed under UK GDPR — [Warner Goodman](https://www.warnergoodman.co.uk/site/blog/news/gdpr-news-update-on-b2b-communications); context in [SCL](https://www.scl.org/10176-b2b-communications-a-welcome-ico-turnaround/)
- Soft opt-in is narrow: miss one condition and the exemption collapses; the Allay case turned on a missing simple way to refuse marketing at sign-up — [Mind the Shop](https://mindtheshop.co.uk/blogs/notes/uk-email-marketing-rules-2026); [ConsentTrail](https://consenttrail.co.uk/blog/ico-email-marketing-fines)
- January 2026 fines: Allay Claims Ltd £120,000 for 4,046,947 marketing texts (Reg 22); ZMLUK Limited £105,000 for 67,772,285 marketing emails without valid consent — consent to a list of 361 "partner" companies was neither informed nor specific — [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/data-protection/spring-2026/ico-issues-fines-over-electronic-marketing-breaches/); [Privacy Core](https://www.privacycoreservices.com/en/insights/ico-enforcement-recent-actions)
- 2025 example: Home Improvement Marketing Ltd MPN dated 28 Aug 2025 for 2,449,380 automated calls without consent (May–Aug 2023) — [ICO notice](https://ico.org.uk/action-weve-taken/enforcement/2025/09/home-improvement-marketing-ltd-monetary-penalty-notice/)
- Aggregate: 49 PECR MPNs for unsolicited marketing since March 2022 totalling £4.63m, average ~£94,490, ~1.4 fines/month (undated analysis) — [GRC Solutions](https://grcsolutions.io/analysing-the-ico-pecr-fines-for-unsolicited-marketing)
- ICO pursuing winding-up petitions to recover unpaid PECR fines (It's OK Ltd, F12 Management, Poxell, Pinnacle Life) — [Decision Marketing](https://www.decisionmarketing.co.uk/?p=127674); ICO executed search warrants in 2026 over nuisance marketing linked to car-finance claims — [Freeths](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2026/ico-executes-search-warrants-to-combat-nuisance-marketing-linked-to-car-finance-mis-selling-claims/)
- DUA Act 2025 (Royal Assent 19 Jun 2025) raises the PECR maximum from £500,000 to £17.5m or 4% of global turnover, aligning with UK GDPR — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/the-data-use-and-access-act-pecr-reform-rules-relating-to-electronic-marketing-and-cookies-in-the-uk); [Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2025/data-protection/data-use-and-access-act-2025-top-7-changes/); [Didomi](https://www.didomi.io/blog/data-use-and-access-act-duaa-2025). Effective date conflicts: two secondary sources say 5 Feb 2026 / February 2026 — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/b2b-email-marketing-rules-uk/); [Mailgraf](https://mailgraf.com/blog/uk-gdpr-email-marketing-guide); a 2026 Freeths article still cites the £500,000 cap — [Freeths](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2026/ico-executes-search-warrants-to-combat-nuisance-marketing-linked-to-car-finance-mis-selling-claims/). Not resolved.
- EU/US recipients: Overloop's 2026 guide states B2B cold email to corporate addresses is legal in the UK while consent is required for sole traders and B2C — [Overloop](https://overloop.com/blog/cold-email-illegal); GDPR Local's B2B guide covers UK GDPR lawful-basis requirements for business contacts — [GDPR Local](https://gdprlocal.com/b2b-gdpr/). (No EU-member-state-by-state or CAN-SPAM detail surfaced in this research.)

### Inferences
- A compliant UK B2B agent pipeline: (1) filter targets to corporate subscribers by legal form (Companies House company type makes this automatable — exclude sole traders/partnerships or route them to a consent path); (2) use a role-relevant, work-email contact with a documented legitimate-interests assessment under UK GDPR; (3) identify the sender and include a one-click unsubscribe on every message (also required by Gmail/Yahoo); (4) honour opt-outs globally across all mailboxes and domains — domain rotation must not reset suppression lists; (5) keep an audit log of source, lawful basis and opt-out per record.
- The ICO's 2025–26 enforcement pattern (millions of consumer texts/emails, bought lists, fake "partner" consent) indicates very low enforcement probability for careful, low-volume B2B outreach to corporates, but the DUAA ceiling and the regulator's new appetite for winding-up and search warrants make list-buying and ignoring opt-outs materially riskier than in 2024.
- For EU recipients, the operator cannot rely on the UK corporate-subscriber exemption; several member states require opt-in for B2B email, so EU targets should be routed to LinkedIn/inbound/partner channels or legal review (inference; no state-level source captured here).

### Gaps
- Could not read the ICO's own electronic-mail-marketing guidance page to confirm the current wording on named individuals at corporate subscribers.
- The commencement date of the DUAA PECR-fine uplift was not confirmed from legislation.gov.uk or the ICO.
- No 2025 or 2026 PECR fine targeting B2B email was found; no complete 2025 fine list was retrieved.
- EU member-state B2B opt-in rules and US CAN-SPAM specifics were not researched (outside the search budget).

## Key Question 5: What a fully automated acquisition pipeline costs per booked meeting and per closed client in 2026 (with three costed designs)

### Takeaway
Self-serve stacks price out at roughly $50–$500 per booked meeting depending on reply rate and how labour is counted (Instantly's own example $79 with $800 of labour; Smartlead's $467 outsourced; agencies $150–$500 per meeting and $2k–$8k/month retainers), against $700–$1,150 for an in-house SDR. With a ~£10k budget, a solo operator can run a 10–20 mailbox signal-triggered email engine plus a self-serve productised entry offer for roughly £400–£700/month in tooling, implying £15–£60 per meeting at median reply rates and £200–£900 per closed client at 20–34% proposal close.

### Cited Findings
- Instantly CPM calculator (updated Nov 2025): CPM = total outbound investment ÷ meetings; example Hypergrowth $97 + SuperSearch $47 + $800 allocated SDR labour = $944 for 12 meetings → $78.67/meeting — [Instantly calculator](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-outbound-cost-per-meeting-calculator/)
- Smartlead: at 0.74% reply, ~$467 per held meeting outsourced — [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/cold-email-agency-pricing)
- Agency pay-per-meeting: $150–$400 ([Reply.io](https://reply.io/blog/lead-generation-pricing-explained/)); $75–$300 ([Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/lead-generation-outsourcing-statistics-2026)); $200–$500 per qualified meeting ([Danish Lead Co](https://danishleadco.io/blog/what-is-the-cost-per-meeting-for-outbound-in-2026)); SMB $150 to enterprise $2,500+ ([LeadHaste](https://leadhaste.com/blog/lead-generation-services-cost-2026))
- In-house SDR fully loaded: $700–$1,150 per meeting — [Reply.io](https://reply.io/blog/lead-generation-pricing-explained/)
- Retainers: cold email $2,000–$8,000/mo; multichannel $5,000–$15,000+ ([Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/b2b-lead-generation-agency-cost)); typical done-for-you $3,000–$7,000/mo with platforms/enrichment billed as pass-through ([LeadHaste](https://leadhaste.com/blog/cold-email-agency-pricing-2026))
- Range across sources: $50–$1,700 per meeting with seniority driving spread; one source claims $1,500–$15,000 per meeting at industry-average rates (outlier) — [Danish Lead Co](https://danishleadco.io/blog/how-much-does-a-cold-email-campaign-cost-in-2026); [Modern Inbound](https://moderninbound.com/blog/cold-email-deal-size-roi-calculator-2026)
- Breakeven: outbound rarely pays below ~$3,000–$5,000 ACV — [Modern Inbound](https://moderninbound.com/blog/cold-email-deal-size-roi-calculator-2026)
- Tooling unit costs (see Q2): Smartlead Pro $94 / Instantly Hypergrowth $97; mailboxes $4.50–9 each or Google Workspace £5.90; domains $13–19/yr; Apollo Basic $49; Exa $7 per 1,000; Parallel $1–5 per 1,000; verification $32/mo; Companies House feeds $2–5 per 1,000 records.
- Funnel inputs (see Q1): 30–50 emails/day per warmed mailbox; 3.43% average reply (0.74% Smartlead conservative; 5.5% top quartile); 0.7% average meeting rate (2–3% top); 20% industry / 34% platform proposal close; 2.5 days to close.

### Inferences — three costed pipeline designs (operator assumptions stated; FX assumption $1 ≈ £0.78)

**Design A — Signal-triggered cold email engine (corporate subscribers only), weekly human gate**
- Stack: Companies House new-incorporation + hiring-signal feeds (£5–15/mo), Apollo Basic for contacts ($49), Exa/Parallel for research (~£20/mo at 3,000 searches), Smartlead Pro ($94) or Instantly Hypergrowth ($97), 12 mailboxes on 4 secondary domains (12 × £5.90 = £71; domains ~£50/yr), verification ($32), LLM drafting (unpriced; see gap). Total ≈ £280–£330/month, ≈ £1,000 one-off for setup, domains, warm-up period (3 weeks of no sends).
- Volume: 12 mailboxes × 30/day × 20 days = 7,200 emails/month (well under the 5,000/day bulk thresholds; stays under Google/Microsoft bulk rules but authenticate anyway).
- Funnel at median (3.43% reply, 0.7% meeting, 25% meeting→proposal→close blended): 247 replies → ~50 meetings → ~12 proposals/closes range 8–17 (20–34% close on ~40 proposals). At conservative (0.74% reply, 0.3% meeting): 53 replies → ~22 meetings → 4–7 closes. These are arithmetic on vendor benchmarks, not observed.
- Cost per meeting ≈ £6–£15 (median) to £15–£40 (conservative) on tooling alone; adding 6 hours/week of operator time at a notional £75/hr (£1,800/mo) gives £35–£90 per meeting and £200–£900 per closed client.
- Human gate (weekly, ~4–6 h): approve the ICP filter and signal list; read and edit the week's AI drafts (addresses the 34% unedited-AI penalty); triage positive replies; take calls; sign off proposals. Everything else (sourcing, enrichment, legal-form filtering, suppression, sequencing, reply classification, proposal drafting, invoicing via Stripe) runs unattended.
- Compliance: filter to corporate subscribers by Companies House company type; identify sender; one-click unsubscribe; global suppression across all domains; LIA on file.

**Design B — Inbound-first with self-serve productised entry offer and partner referrals**
- Stack: programmatic/comparison content generated and fact-checked by agents, structured for AI-answer citation; Product Hunt/community launches; Stripe Payment Links + Stripe Tax for a fixed-price audit or single-workflow build (2.9% + 30c analogue; UK rate unverified); 2–3 agency referral partners at 15–25% revenue share; Firecrawl/Exa for research (~£30/mo); hosting/analytics (~£30/mo). Total ≈ £80–£150/month plus partner payouts on success only.
- Funnel: volume is the constraint — AI search is <2% of B2B search referrals. If content earns 300 organic + 30 AI-referred visits/month, benchmark conversion (2.8% organic, 14% AI-referred, both vendor-claimed) gives ~8 + ~4 = ~12 leads/month; at 12% sales-assisted trial→paid analogue, ~1–2 self-serve purchases/month, plus whatever partners pass. Expect 3–6 months before content ranks. Cost per lead ≈ £10–£15 but cost per closed client depends almost entirely on operator content time.
- Human gate: weekly publication approval, partner calls, fulfilment of the paid entry product. Agents handle research, drafting, schema/structuring, review-response, checkout, onboarding emails and narrative monthly reports (the 9% vs 31% churn lever).

**Design C — Hybrid: Design A email engine + human-run LinkedIn + post-reply spec work**
- Adds: Sales Navigator (~£80–£100/mo, price unverified here), 100 manual connection requests/week (within the widely reported rolling limit), Loom recorded after a positive reply (not before), proposal tool with e-sign and embedded video (Proposify/PandaDoc ~£40–£80/mo). Total ≈ £450–£550/month.
- Expected lift (vendor claims only): multichannel ~50% more replies; LinkedIn+email ~15% combined reply vs ~10% alone; video-in-proposal 3.3x close likelihood. Applying a conservative +30% to Design A's median meetings gives ~65 meetings and 12–22 closes/month at a cost per close of ~£150–£700 including operator time (~10 h/week).
- Human gate: all LinkedIn actions manual (ToS); weekly review as in A; record Looms only for the shortlist of positive replies.

**Budget fit (£10k):** Design A or C consumes £4k–£7k in year one including a 3-week warm-up with zero output, leaving headroom for a second domain cluster if spam-rate signals stay under 0.1%. The £3,000–$5,000 ACV breakeven rule means the productised entry offer should be priced ≥£1,500 or paired with a retainer upsell (retainer agencies churn 1.6%/month vs 4.2% for project work).

### Gaps
- LLM API cost per personalised email (research + draft) was not sourced; this can be a material line at 7,000+ emails/month.
- No source provides an observed, end-to-end solo-operator pipeline with cost per closed client; all per-close figures above are arithmetic on vendor benchmarks.
- Sales Navigator UK pricing, Proposify/PandaDoc 2026 pricing, and Stripe/Paddle UK fee schedules were not confirmed.
- Operator case studies with numbers from Indie Hackers or r/coldemail did not surface in the searches run; the "operator" numbers in this note are vendor-published.
