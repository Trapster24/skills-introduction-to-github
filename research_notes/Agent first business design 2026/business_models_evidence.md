# Agent-first and AI-native business models run profitably by solo operators / very small teams (evidence file, as of October 2026)

**Method and sourcing caveat.** Research was conducted via web search only. Page fetching (WebFetch and curl) was blocked by the sandbox proxy for every external host attempted (onepc.org, forbes.com, saastr.com, thewitn.com, techcrunch.com), so every finding below rests on search-result extracts of the cited page rather than a full read of it. Primary-source numbers that could be confirmed only through secondary write-ups are labelled as such. "Self-reported" = founder tweet/dashboard/podcast; "verified" = acquisition price confirmed by acquirer, Stripe-connected leaderboard, or regulator-grade reporting. Anything from before 2025 is marked **[dated]**.

---

## Key Question 0: Model-by-model evidence ledger (what is sold, who buys, price points, margin, time to first revenue, human time per £, what breaks, named examples)

### Takeaway
Across nine model families, only three have multiple named operators with revenue figures that are better than a founder tweet: (a) micro-SaaS / AI product wrappers (Base44 acquisition confirmed by Wix; Stripe-connected leaderboard data), (b) done-for-you automation agencies (consistent UK price surveys, but almost no named solo P&Ls), and (c) outcome-priced customer-service agents (public per-resolution prices, but these are venture-scale vendors, not solo operators). Agent-run e-commerce/arbitrage, productised research, localisation and vertical agents have abundant hypothetical "$20K–$80K MRR" playbooks and almost no documented solo cases.

## Key Question 3: Which models are commoditising fastest in 2026 (platform features eating them) and which are durable?

### Takeaway
Fastest-commoditising: thin wrappers and single-feature chatbots (ChatGPT absorbing personal finance, document search, meeting notes, connectors; Operator folded into ChatGPT; Relevance marketplace shipping invoice-extraction templates; Clay/Instantly DIY stacks beating agencies on cost per meeting), plus AI SDR after the 11x credibility collapse. More durable per the evidence: workflow-embedded automation tied to systems of record, vertical niches enterprise vendors skip, and products with proprietary data loops — though even "wrappers die" is contested (Jasper reportedly projecting $180M for 2026).

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI previewed a personal-finance experience inside ChatGPT (15 May 2026); earlier it added native document search, meeting-note capture and connectors in ChatGPT Business; Operator was folded back into ChatGPT; OpenAI's Sora app shut down April 2026. — [quasa.io](https://quasa.io/media/meanwhile-openai-just-killed-another-hundred-startups); [firstaimovers](https://www.firstaimovers.com/p/openai-kill-start-ups); [venturecurator](https://www.venturecurator.com/p/ai-startups-model-release-risk)
- Counter-evidence: Jasper "was a wrapper and it's still here, projecting $180M for 2026". The oft-repeated "200+ GPT-wrapper startups killed in 2024" statistic has no traceable primary source. — [venturecurator](https://www.venturecurator.com/p/ai-startups-model-release-risk)
- Features like PDF parsing, basic RAG and conversational memory are increasingly built into model-provider APIs; what needed GPT-4-class models a year ago runs on fine-tuned open-source models at a fraction of the cost. — [founderventures](https://founderventures.io/intel/ai-wrapper-churn-vertical-saas-moats-2026); [aimagicx](https://www.aimagicx.com/blog/saaspocalypse-software-categories-ai-eliminating-2026)

- Specialised (vertical) agents reportedly show 3–5× higher retention than horizontal ones (unsourced claim). — [saasmag](https://www.saasmag.com/vertical-ai-agents-eating-horizontal-saas/)

### Inferences
- The commoditisation pattern is consistent: wherever the deliverable is a prompt plus a public API (chatbot, summariser, lead list, invoice extraction), the model vendor or workflow platform ships it as a feature within 6–18 months. - The first 90 days in these cases were spent on launch distribution, not on sales calls; this is the opposite of the agency path and is the reason the agency path has no comparable public case studies.

### Gaps
- No one-person company past $1M ARR was found in the UK or in agency/services models.
- Deeper first-90-day detail (channels, spend, conversion) was not retrievable because page fetches were blocked.
